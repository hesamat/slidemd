<!-- variant: story -->

Use a narrative, story-driven approach. Frame the material around characters, situations, or examples the audience can relate to. Build tension with a problem or conflict, then deliver a clear payoff. Use before/after framing and concrete scenes rather than abstract assertions.

<!-- variant: technical -->

Use a technical, logic-driven approach. Lead with evidence, data, or precise terminology. Build complexity step by step, introducing concepts before examples. Use code, architecture, or real-system illustrations to ground explanations.

<!-- variant: persuasive -->

Use a persuasive, argument-driven approach. Establish the problem and stakes first, present evidence and the solution, then close with benefits and a clear call to action. Anticipate objections and address them with concrete proof.

<!-- variant: instructional -->

Use an instructional, learning-driven approach. State objectives up front, then provide step-by-step guidance with examples and likely mistakes. Include a brief recap or practice cue to reinforce learning.
